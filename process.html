From earth to jewel

A short, plain-language look at how gemstones come to exist, leave the ground, and end up in a ring or pendant. Educational mockup

How gems form

Most gems are minerals that crystallized deep underground (or in a few cases, cooled from molten rock). Heat, pressure, and chemistry over thousands or millions of years arrange atoms into orderly structures — that internal order is what gives many gems their brilliance when cut.

Organic gems like pearl, coral, and amber form through biological or fossil processes rather than deep-earth crystals, but they are still treasured for beauty and rarity.

Mining & recovery

Rough material may come from large industrial mines, small artisanal pits, or alluvial deposits where rivers have washed stones downstream. Responsible sourcing considers worker safety, environmental impact, and transparent supply chains — topics we are happy to discuss for any piece you are considering.

The path to finished jewelry

Rough sorting

Miners and dealers separate material by species, color, and potential clarity. Some rough travels with paperwork documenting origin.

Cutting & polishing

Lapidaries plan facets to maximize color, weight, and sparkle. A skilled cutter can rescue a challenging piece or ruin a great one — craft matters.

Treatments & disclosure

Many gems are heated, oiled, or otherwise treated to improve appearance. Industry ethics require disclosure; we believe customers should always know what they are buying.

Design & setting

Jewelers build mountings that protect the stone, suit daily wear, and express personal style — the final step before a piece reaches our cases.
